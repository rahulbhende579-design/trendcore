1. Stock Market Basics

Foundational pillars of the Indian financial ecosystem

What is a Stock?

A stock (or share/equity) represents fractional ownership in a corporation. When you buy a share of Reliance or TCS, you literally own a piece of that enterprise and participate in its growth through capital gains and dividends.

What is NSE?

The National Stock Exchange (NSE) is India’s largest electronic financial exchange located in Mumbai. Established in 1992, it pioneered modern computerized screen-based trading and is known worldwide for massive derivative volumes.

What is BSE?

The Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE), founded in 1875 at Dalal Street, is Asia's oldest stock exchange. Over 5,000 companies are listed on BSE, offering unparalleled corporate breadth and trading heritage.

What is NIFTY 50?

The NIFTY 50 is the flagship benchmark index of the NSE. It tracks the weighted performance of the top 50 blue-chip Indian companies across 13 economic sectors, acting as the pulse of the Indian economy.

What is SENSEX?

The S&P BSE SENSEX is a basket of 30 financially sound, heavily traded companies listed on the BSE. It is calculated using free-float market capitalization methodology and reflects overall market sentiment.

Indian Market Hours

  • Pre-market: 09:00 AM - 09:15 AM
  • Regular Trading: 09:15 AM - 03:30 PM
  • Post-market: 03:40 PM - 04:00 PM
  • Trading days: Monday to Friday (excluding national holidays)

2. Trading Basics & Order Types

Mastering market execution, order mechanics, and trade durations

Market Order

Instant Fill

An instruction to buy or sell immediately at the best available current market price. Prioritizes execution speed over price certainty.

When to use: When you need your trade filled right now and the stock has high trading liquidity.

Limit Order

Price Control

An order to buy at or below a specified target price, or sell at or above a target price. Guarantees price but does not guarantee execution if the market never touches your target.

When to use: When you are waiting for a pullback or refuse to buy above a disciplined ceiling.

Stop Loss (SL)

A conditional safety order designed to limit an investor's loss on an existing position. If the stock drops to your predetermined trigger price, the order activates to automatically exit the trade and protect your remaining capital.

Golden Rule: Never enter a short-term trade without knowing your stop-loss exit in advance.

Intraday vs. Delivery

Intraday (MIS): Positions entered and squared off on the exact same trading day before 03:20 PM.

Delivery (CNC): Shares purchased and held in your Demat account for multiple days, months, or years for long-term wealth creation.

3. Investment Basics & Risk Management

Strategies for sustainable, long-term capital compounding

Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)

SIP allows investing a fixed sum periodically (e.g. monthly) into mutual funds or stocks. It enforces financial discipline and leverages Rupee Cost Averaging (buying more units when prices dip and fewer when prices peak).

Mutual Funds

A pooled investment vehicle managed by professional Asset Management Companies (AMCs) and SEBI-registered fund managers who allocate capital across diverse equities and debt securities.

Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)

Marketable securities that track an underlying index (like NIFTYBEES for NIFTY 50). ETFs trade live on exchanges just like regular stocks with ultra-low expense ratios and high transparency.

Portfolio Diversification

The technique of spreading investments across sectors (IT, Banking, FMCG, Energy) to reduce non-systematic risk. If one sector undergoes a cyclical downturn, other sectors mitigate the impact.

Disciplined Risk Management

In trading, capital preservation always precedes profit maximization. Key guidelines:

  • The 1-2% Rule: Never risk more than 1-2% of your total portfolio on any single trade.
  • Risk-to-Reward Ratio: Only take trades with at least a 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio.
  • Emotional Detachment: Never let fear or greed override your trading thesis.
